# Emerging Markets Macro Risk Monitoring Dashboard
A Python-based portfolio risk monitoring prototype combining financial market data and macroeconomic indicators to analyse risk exposure across emerging markets.

# Overview
This project examines portfolio exposures across emerging-market countries, sectors and asset classes, with a focus on the relationship between macroeconomic conditions and financial market risk.

The dashboard combines portfolio risk analytics with country-level macroeconomic indicators to assess concentration, identify overlapping risk exposures and evaluate the potential impact of adverse market conditions. The analysis will incorporate indicators such as foreign direct investment (FDI), inflation, interest rates, foreign exchange movements and external financing conditions.

The portfolio will use hypothetical allocations alongside real market data, providing a framework for analysing cross-market exposures and conducting scenario-based stress tests.

# Key Components
1. Portfolio Exposure Analysis: Asset allocation by country, sector, asset class and currency.
2. Concentration Monitoring: Identification of concentrated positions and common risk exposures across markets.
3. Portfolio Risk Analytics: Volatility, correlation analysis, drawdowns, Value at Risk (VaR) and Expected Shortfall.
4. Macroeconomic Monitoring: Analysis of FDI inflows, inflation, GDP growth, foreign exchange reserves and other indicators of external vulnerability.
5. Scenario Analysis & Stress Testing: Assessment of portfolio sensitivity to currency depreciation, interest rate shocks, commodity price movements and changes in global financial conditions.

# Tools
1. Python: Data processing and quantitative analysis
2. Google Colab: Notebook-based development and experimentation
3. Pandas & NumPy: Data manipulation and portfolio risk calculations
4. yfinance and APIs: Market and macroeconomic data retrieval, subject to availability
5. Plotly & Streamlit: Interactive visualisation and dashboard development

# Data Sources
1. Market Data: Historical asset prices and exchange rates from suitable market-data providers.
2. Macroeconomic Data: World Bank and IMF databases for indicators including FDI, inflation, GDP growth and balance-of-payments statistics.
